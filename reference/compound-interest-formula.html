The Compound Interest Formula and Its Variables

Formula Shelf Reference Desk · Definition page · Reviewed October 2026

Definition

A = P(1+r)^t for lump sums, plus the separate future-value formula for regular monthly contributions.

A = P × (1 + r)^t · Regular contributions: FV = C × (((1+i)^n − 1) ÷ i)
A / FVFuture value
PStarting principal
rAnnual return or rate, as a decimal
tYears
CContribution per period
iRate per period
nNumber of periods

Worked example

A lump sum of $2,000 at 5% for 10 years: A = 2,000 × 1.05^10 ≈ $3,258. Adding $100 monthly at the same annual rate (i ≈ 0.004167, n = 120) gives FV ≈ $15,528 for the contributions stream alone.

Read this before using it

Both formulas assume a constant rate. Real returns vary year to year, so treat outputs as planning illustrations. The formulas are still exact for fixed-rate products such as many savings certificates.
