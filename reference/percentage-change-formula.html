Percentage Change Formula: Increase, Decrease, and the Base Rule

Formula Shelf Reference Desk · Definition page · Reviewed October 2026

Definition

The one formula behind price rises, discounts, growth rates and declines — and the base-value rule that prevents most errors.

Change % = (New − Old) ÷ Old × 100
IncreaseResult is positive
DecreaseResult is negative
Percentage pointsThe plain difference between two percentages — not a percentage change

Worked example

A salary rising from $48,000 to $51,600 changed by (3,600 ÷ 48,000) × 100 = +7.5%. A rate moving from 4% to 5% rose by 1 percentage point, which is a 25% relative increase — news reports often blur these two, and they are not the same claim.

Read this before using it

The denominator is always the OLD value. Dividing by the new value is the single most common percentage error in spreadsheets and headlines alike.
